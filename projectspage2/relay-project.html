Relay terminals explained

The relay board has two sets of connections — the high-voltage/current side that switches your load, and the low-voltage control side that plugs into your board.

⚡ Load side
COM Common. Connect your power source here — this is the shared connection point.
NO Normally Open. Circuit is open (off) by default. Closes when control pin is set LOW.
NC Normally Closed. Circuit is closed (on) by default. Opens when control pin is set HIGH.
🎛️ Control side
VCC Power for the board. Accepts 3.3V or 5V from your development board.
GND Ground. Connect to GND on your development board.
IN1/IN2 Control signals. One pin per relay channel — connect to a GPIO pin on your board.
JD-VCC Coil power. Normally jumpered to VCC. Remove jumper to use an isolated external supply for the relay coils.

Understanding the JD-VCC jumper

✔ Jumper on (default)

Shared power supply

JD-VCC is connected to VCC. The relay coils and the control logic share the same 5V supply. Fine for most small DC loads and learning projects.

✦ Jumper removed

Isolated power supply

Connect an external supply (e.g. 12V) directly to JD-VCC. Better electrical isolation protects the microcontroller from the high-voltage load side. Use this for AC or larger loads.

🔊 If you hear buzzing or notice erratic relay behavior, your board's 5V supply may not be strong enough to drive the coils. Remove the jumper and power JD-VCC from a separate supply.

Wiring & safety

⚠️ Safety first. If connecting to a wall power receptacle or any 120V AC circuit, always make all other connections first and unplug the power last. Contact with line voltage can be deadly. For learning, start with a small DC load like an LED or battery-powered motor.

For a basic test with your development board, connect three wires from the relay's control side:

WireFrom relayTo board
Signal IN1 Pin 13
Power VCC 5V (or 3.3V)
Ground GND GND

AC load wiring

When switching an AC device (like a lamp) the line wire from the receptacle passes through the relay. Use the isolated JD-VCC configuration for better protection.

💡 Use the NO terminal if you want the device OFF by default and ON when triggered. Use NC if you want it ON by default and OFF when triggered.
5

Arduino sketch

Controlling a relay is as simple as toggling a pin. This sketch alternates the relay on and off every 3 seconds — you'll hear a click each time it switches.

💬 Whether the circuit is active on LOW or HIGH depends on which terminal you used. NC is active when the pin is LOW; NO is active when the pin is HIGH. Choose based on your wiring.
Arduino C++ 
#define RelayPin 13

void setup() {
    // Set RelayPin as an output pin
    pinMode(RelayPin, OUTPUT);
}

void loop() {
    // Turn ON the relay (NC terminal active)
    digitalWrite(RelayPin, LOW);
    delay(3000);

    // Turn OFF the relay
    digitalWrite(RelayPin, HIGH);
    delay(3000);
}

Upload the sketch and you should hear the relay clicking on and off every 3 seconds. If you have a small load connected, it will switch on and off with the relay.
